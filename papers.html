  • Agreeing with yourself
    Aumann's (1976) agreement theorem is proved here from a more elementary construction. Start with one algebra and one probability measure. Represent two cases that differ in which events have probability 0 by two cells of a partition of that measure, not by two measures and not by two stages of an updating process. Conditional probabilities are ratios already defined in the measure. If the cells give an event the same ratio, finite additivity gives the event that ratio unconditionally, whatever the cells' weights. The same single sum works for E and ¬E, and directly for any finite partition E₁, …, Eₙ, without induction. With the event definitions held fixed and the learned cell certain, the same construction gives Bayesian conditionalization at a later point, for every event at once. Conditionalization is then a calculation of a value the measure already has, not a rule for updating it. A second identity, the one-constant law, explains which ratios survive conditioning and why two cells agree on every ratio inside their overlap. Aumann's theorem follows by applying the finite-partition sum inside the common-knowledge event M, once for each agent's partition. Common knowledge supplies M and the constancy of the reported posteriors within it, and the remaining computation uses only one measure, ratios, additivity and positivity. The agents are then cells of one measure, and the agreement is agreement with yourself. Since Bayesian updating is a calculation, the result needs no common knowledge of rationality, on which Aumann's theorem implicitly depends, and no substantive assumptions that are not stated in the proof. What the theorem needs from learning is that what each agent conditions on is certain and true. The paper shows that in reasonable price-discovery situations these assumptions do not hold, and there a model can be given in which the theorem's conclusion fails: two people with a common prior and common knowledge veer apart. One measure cannot. Jeffrey's rule, which is order-dependent, would allow agreeing to disagree. But the rule is disproved, so it is no obstacle to the result.
